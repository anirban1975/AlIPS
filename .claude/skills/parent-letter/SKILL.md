# Parent Letter

Parents skim. They read on a phone, between other things, often in a
WhatsApp group where your message is competing with forty others. A letter
that buries the ask in paragraph three has failed regardless of how well
it is written.

## Before drafting, make sure you have the six facts

Never draft around a missing fact — ask, or leave a marked blank.

1. **What** is happening
2. **When** — date and time, written out (`Friday, 12 September 2026, 9:00 AM`)
3. **Where** — venue, with a landmark if it is not the school
4. **Who** it applies to — which classes, which parents
5. **What the parent must do** — and by when
6. **Who to contact** with questions — name and number

If the user has not given you one of these, write `[NEED: venue]` inline
rather than inventing it. A blank is fixable; a wrong date is a crisis.

## Structure

```
School letterhead line (name, address, affiliation)
Ref. no. and date
Subject line — specific, under 10 words

Dear Parents / Dear Parents and Guardians,

  Paragraph 1: The news or the ask. First sentence, no warm-up.
  Paragraph 2: The details — the six facts, in a bulleted block if
               there is more than one date or requirement.
  Paragraph 3: What the parent needs to do, and the deadline.
  Paragraph 4 (optional): One line of warmth or context.

Sincerely / Warm regards,
[Name], [Designation]
[Contact]
```

## Rules of craft

- **One letter, one purpose.** If the user asks for fee reminder + Sports
  Day + uniform change in one letter, push back: three letters, or one
  letter with three clearly boxed sections. Mixed-purpose letters get
  half-read and the fee reminder gets missed.
- **Lead with the ask.** "The last date for fee payment is 15 September"
  beats "As you are aware, the school follows a quarterly fee cycle..."
- **Active voice, human subject.** "We will hold the PTM" not "A PTM will
  be conducted." Institutional passive makes schools sound like the
  municipal water department.
- **Under 250 words** unless it is a policy change. If it is longer, ask
  yourself what you can move to a bullet.
- **Dates always carry the weekday.** Parents plan by weekday.
- **Never open with "It is hereby informed that"** or "This is to bring to
  your kind notice." Delete these on sight.
- **Bad news goes early and plainly.** Softening a fee increase across four
  paragraphs reads as evasive and makes parents angrier, not less.

## Always produce both versions

After the formal letter, add:

```
---
WHATSAPP VERSION (under 400 characters)
---
```

The WhatsApp version is not a summary — it is the same message rewritten
for a phone screen. Emoji sparingly and only for events, never for fees,
discipline, or bad news. Keep the date, the action, and the contact. Drop
the salutation and the letterhead.

## Tone by letter type

| Type | Tone | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Event invitation | Warm, inviting | Don't bury the RSVP deadline |
| Fee reminder | Neutral, factual, never shaming | Never imply the family is delinquent; some are struggling |
| Disciplinary | Grave, specific, non-accusatory to the parent | Describe behaviour, not character. Never label the child |
| Achievement | Proud but not boastful | Name the child only with consent — check the hard rules |
| Apology | Direct, own it in sentence one | No "mistakes were made"; say what we did and what we will do |
| Policy change | Clear, with the reason | State the effective date in bold; give a transition period |

## After the draft

End your reply by telling the user, in one line, what you left blank and
what you assumed — so they can correct you before it goes out. Never send
the letter yourself; the user sends it.
