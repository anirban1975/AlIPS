# Social Post

School social media has one job during ten months of the year — build
familiarity and trust so that during the other two months, admission
enquiries arrive warm. Posts that only shout "ADMISSIONS OPEN" to an
audience that has never seen you convert nothing.

## Check the hard rules first

Before writing anything featuring a student, re-read the hard rules in
`CLAUDE.md`. Face + full name + class together in public material requires
written consent on file. If the user has not confirmed consent, write the
caption using first name only or "a Class VIII student", and flag it:
`[CONSENT CHECK: confirm before posting with the photo]`.

Never invent a result, rank, award, percentage, or testimonial. Use
`[NEED: exact figure]`.

## Ask before writing

- Which platform (changes everything — see the table)
- What is the post about
- Is there a photo/video already, or does artwork need creating
- Is there a call to action, or is this a trust-building post

## The nine post types that actually work for schools

| Type | What it does | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| **Student work** | Proof of learning, not claims of it | Weekly |
| **Teacher feature** | Parents choose teachers, not buildings | Fortnightly |
| **Behind the scenes** | Lab prep, library, kitchen, ground — builds trust | Weekly |
| **Achievement** | Board results, competitions, sports | As they happen |
| **Event coverage** | Annual Day, Sports Day, festivals | As they happen |
| **Facility** | The lab, the bus, the playground, the safety measures | Monthly |
| **Alumni** | Where our students went — the strongest proof there is | Monthly |
| **Educational value** | A study tip, an exam-stress note for parents | Weekly |
| **Admissions** | The direct ask | Heavy only in season |

**The 80/20 rule:** at most one in five posts asks for something. The other
four give something — proof, insight, warmth, or usefulness.

## Platform rules

| | Instagram | Facebook | WhatsApp | LinkedIn |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Audience | Younger parents, students | Parents 30-55, the largest school audience in most Indian cities | Existing parents, high trust | Educators, partners, hiring |
| Length | 125 chars before "more" — front-load it | 1-2 short paras, can be longer | Under 300 chars | 2-4 paras, professional |
| Hashtags | 8-15 | 2-3 | None | 3-5 |
| Tone | Visual-first, warm | Conversational, community | Direct, personal | Institutional, credible |
| Best for | Events, student work | Everything — this is the workhorse | Reminders, urgent, results | Recruitment, affiliations |

## Writing the caption

**Line one is the whole game.** It is the only line most people read.
It must do one of: ask a question, state a surprising fact, name a
specific person, or open a story mid-scene.

- Dead: "AlIPS celebrated Science Day with great enthusiasm."
- Alive: "Aarav built a working hydraulic lift out of syringes and tape."

Then: 2-3 short lines of substance. Then the CTA if there is one. Then
hashtags on their own line at the bottom.

**Specificity beats adjectives.** "Great enthusiasm", "grand celebration",
"immense joy", "a memorable day" — these are filler that every school
posts. Cut every one and replace with a detail: the number, the name, the
thing that actually happened.

**Write for the parent, not about the school.** "Your child will..." lands;
"Our institution provides..." does not.

## Hashtags

Mix three kinds: location (`#SchoolsIn[City]`, `#[City]`), category
(`#CBSESchool`, `#Admissions2027`), and brand (`#AlIPS`). Location tags
matter most — parents search locally. Skip the generic
`#education #school #learning` mass; they reach nobody.

## Always deliver

1. The caption, ready to paste
2. Hashtag block, separate
3. **A visual brief** — what the photo or artwork should show, in one or
   two sentences. Offer to build it in Canva.
4. Best posting time, if relevant (school audiences: 7-9 AM and 8-10 PM)

## Admission season

During the admission window named in `CLAUDE.md`, shift the ratio to
roughly 60/40 give-to-ask, and make sure every post has a clear next step
— a phone number, a form link, or "DM us". Outside that window, go back to
80/20. Do not quote fees; direct to the admissions desk.

## Never

- Post a comparative claim about another school
- Use stock photos of foreign children as if they were our students
- Recycle a competitor's caption
- Promise outcomes ("guaranteed 90%+")
- Publish exam results with student photos and marks together without
  explicit written consent from that family
